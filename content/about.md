Let's Tango helps companies adopt agentic development: a way of building software where people decide what to build and judge the result, and AI agents do more of the building. We also build [Stride](/stride/), the kanban board where people and AI agents share the work.

We're based in Toronto, Ontario, Canada. We consult with companies throughout North America and support Stride customers around the world.

## From continuous delivery to agentic development

Let's Tango started in 2016 helping organizations get to continuous delivery and thriving businesses. That work turned out to be the foundation for what we do now.

AI agents are only as good as the system around them. Automated tests, fast pipelines, small batches of work and quality gates are what let a team trust code it didn't type, and they're exactly what continuous delivery teams already have. Agentic development is the next step on the same road, and we help teams take it.

## How we think

**People decide, agents build.** People own the intent and the judgement: what to build, why, and whether the result is right. Agents do the building, inside clear limits that people set.

**Start with requirements, not code.** An agent builds what it's told, and fills gaps with guesses. Most of the accuracy in agent-built work is decided before any code is written, so we start every piece of work with clear goals, constraints and acceptance criteria. [Read more about how we start](https://cheezyworld.ca/post/half-day-session/).

**Small teams that can see everything.** When agents do the building, a very small team can deliver far more than a traditional one, as long as the agent can see and change every part of the system at once. [Read about scaling the new team](https://cheezyworld.ca/post/scaling-the-new-team/) and [why your agent needs a monorepo](https://cheezyworld.ca/post/monorepo-for-agents/).

**Quality gates over rituals.** Automated checks that run on every change catch more than a queue of pull requests waiting for a reviewer. Review by people still matters, but for the work that actually needs it. [Read about the future of code reviews](https://cheezyworld.ca/post/code-reviews/).
